Audio vs MIDI output

Play: knows synths and loops

OUTPUT MODE decides what the XY pad is: an instrument or a controller.

Audio Output: an instrument

Section titled “Audio Output: an instrument”

The pad plays the app’s own synth. Notes go through the waveform, the Input Mixer, the effects chain and the mixer, and come out of your speakers. Everything in the app makes sound.

MIDI Output: a controller

Section titled “MIDI Output: a controller”

The pad stops making sound itself. Instead, it sends MIDI: messages that say “play this note this hard” and “move this control to here”. Another instrument receives them and makes the sound: a synth app, a DAW, or hardware.

That changes what the pad’s parts do:

Part In audio mode In MIDI mode
X axis Picks the note; moves X mappings Picks the note. X mappings aren’t sent
Y axis Velocity; moves Y mappings Velocity; each Y mapping becomes a MIDI CC message
Audio Effects Shape the synth Don’t affect what’s sent. Map the receiving instrument to the CC numbers instead
Synth arpeggiator, Diatonic Chords Play the synth Send their notes as MIDI
Loop banks Record audio Record MIDI notes and play them to the port
Drums Play Still play in the browser, through the app’s audio

Why use MIDI

Section titled “Why use MIDI”
  • Better sounds. The receiving instrument can be any synth you own.
  • Recording into a DAW. MIDI notes can be edited after you record them: fix a note, change the sound, quantize the timing.
  • The pad as a controller. The scale lock and multi-touch make it a playable controller for instruments that don’t have one.

Why stay with audio

Section titled “Why stay with audio”

Everything is in one place, nothing needs connecting, and the effects mapping is part of the performance. The browser support page lists where MIDI works; on iPhone, iPad and Safari, audio is the only option.
