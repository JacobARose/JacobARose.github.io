Drums

Level: Intermediate

The Drum Kits panel is a 4×4 grid of sample pads (a drum sampler) with a 16-step sequencer. The first three recipes build on each other: play the pads, program a pattern, then loop part of it. The groups after them are independent. Drum kits and sequencer in the reference lists every control.

Play and program

Section titled “Play and program”

1. Play the pads

Section titled “1. Play the pads”

  1. On the left edge, tap the vertical DRUM KITS title to open the panel. If another panel was open, it may close to make room.

  2. Tap or click a pad. Pad 1 is bottom left and pad 16 is top right. Where you hit a pad sets how hard it plays: higher on the pad is harder.

  3. Hit several pads at once with several fingers. Each plays its own sound.

From the keyboard: each pad has a key, laid out like the grid.

2. Program a pattern in the sequencer

Section titled “2. Program a pattern in the sequencer”

  1. In the Drum Kits header, tap PAD. It changes to SEQ and the panel shows the step sequencer.

  2. In SELECT INSTRUMENT, choose a pad, such as Kick.

  3. Tap steps in the grid to turn them on. The 16 steps are one bar of sixteenth notes, read row by row, so each row is one beat. Drag across steps to draw several at once.

  4. Choose another instrument and add its steps. A dot in the list marks each pad that has steps.

  5. Press ▶ in the Drum Kits header to play the pattern on its own, or the global Play in the top bar to play it with the loops. It follows the BPM.

Turn on AUDITION to hear each sound as you add its step. The sequencer keeps playing when you tap SEQ to go back to the pads, so you can play the pads over your own pattern.

3. Loop part of the pattern

Section titled “3. Loop part of the pattern”

The DIVIDE and MOVE knobs under the instrument list loop a window inside the 16 steps. Steps outside the window are dimmed.

  • DIVIDE sets the window’s length: 4 beats (all 16 steps), 2 beats, 1 beat, 0.5 beat or 0.25 beat (a single step).
  • MOVE sets the step where the window starts, from Step 1 to Step 16.

Use a short window to stutter one beat of the pattern, then turn DIVIDE back to 4 beats to release it.

Play with the arpeggiator

Section titled “Play with the arpeggiator”

The Drums arpeggiator repeats the pads you hold at a steady speed, in time with the clock. It’s separate from the Synth arpeggiator, with its own on/off, speed and gate. Open the Arpeggiator panel by tapping its vertical ARPEGGIATOR title on the right edge. Timing rules are in Arpeggiator.

Roll one pad

Section titled “Roll one pad”

  1. In the Arpeggiator panel, select the Drums tab at the bottom, then tap Arp. The dot on the tab lights up.

  2. In the pad view, hold down one pad, such as a closed hat. It repeats at the drum arpeggiator’s speed until you let go.

  3. With Speed selected, drag the slider to 1/16 for a fast roll, or 1/4 for one hit a beat.

Arpeggiate several pads

Section titled “Arpeggiate several pads”

Hold two or more pads. The arpeggiator plays them one at a time, in the order you pressed them, and repeats the cycle. Unlike the synth arpeggiator, it doesn’t sort them.

Keep a roll going with HOLD

Section titled “Keep a roll going with HOLD”

  1. Tap HOLD in the Drum Kits header. It’s separate from the XY pad’s HOLD in the top bar.

  2. Tap the pads you want in the pattern. Each stays latched after you lift your finger, and the arpeggiator keeps playing them.

  3. Tap HOLD again to release them all.

Latched pads play in the order you let go of them. If you press two pads and release them in the other order, they play in the release order.

Lock the drum and synth arpeggiators together

Section titled “Lock the drum and synth arpeggiators together”

Tap the padlock between the Synth and Drums tabs. It turns amber, and both arpeggiators share one timing grid, so their steps land together wherever their speeds meet. Each keeps its own speed and gate.

Shape the kit

Section titled “Shape the kit”

Edit a pad’s sound

Section titled “Edit a pad’s sound”

  1. In the Drum Kits header, tap EDIT. The button turns yellow and reads LOCK: the pads are in edit mode.

  2. Tap a pad. Its editor opens as a floating window you can drag out of the way.

  3. Change any of these. Drag a knob up or down.

    Setting What it does Range
    Name The label on the pad Any text
    VOL How loud the pad is 0 to 200%
    PAN Left to right placement −1 (left) to 1 (right)
    PITCH Tune the sample up or down −24 to +24 semitones
    DECAY How long the sound rings before it’s cut 0.1 to 4 s
    Choke Pads in the same group cut each other off None, 1 to 8

  4. Tap the play button in the editor to hear the pad with its new settings. Close the editor with ×.

  5. Tap LOCK to leave edit mode.

Pad edits are remembered for each kit, factory kits included, so they’re still there after a reload. To keep a factory kit unchanged, SAVE it as your own kit first and edit that. Undoing edits to a factory kit means clearing the app’s data.

Choke groups copy how a real hi-hat works: closing the hat stops the open hat ringing. Put the open and closed hats in the same group and each one cuts the other off.

Load your own sample

Section titled “Load your own sample”

  1. In edit mode, open a pad’s editor.

  2. Next to Sample, tap LOAD and choose an audio file. Any format your browser can play works; WAV and MP3 are safe choices.

  3. The pad now plays your sample, and its file name shows next to Sample.

Loaded samples don't survive a reload

A sample you load is kept only until the page reloads. Saving or exporting the kit doesn’t store the audio itself, only a temporary link to it. After a reload, load the file onto the pad again.

Rearrange the pads

Section titled “Rearrange the pads”

In edit mode, press a pad and drag it onto another one. The two swap places. The layout is remembered for that kit.

Glue the kit together

Section titled “Glue the kit together”

The GLUE knob in the Drum Kits header is a compressor on the whole drum kit. Turn it up to squeeze the hits together so the samples sit closer in level. Turn it down for a more open, dynamic sound.

Workspace

Section titled “Workspace”

Mute or clear the sequencer

Section titled “Mute or clear the sequencer”
  • Mute (the speaker button): silences the sequencer while it keeps running. Unmute it to bring the pattern back in time.
  • Clear instrument: clears the selected instrument’s steps.
  • Clear All: clears every instrument’s steps.

Caution

Clear All doesn’t ask first.

Make the Drum Kits panel bigger

Section titled “Make the Drum Kits panel bigger”

The maximize button in the Drum Kits header fills the screen with the panel, which gives bigger pads and a wider sequencer. Tap it again to go back.
