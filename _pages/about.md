I am the Technology and Digital Pedagogy Coordinator at the NYU Institute of Human Development and Social Change, where I support the NYU Prison Education Program's work to close the digital literacy divide in college-in-prison education.

The work runs along two tracks. On the infrastructure side, I assess and document the technology that incarcerated students actually have access to — offline intranet systems, computer lab hardware, digital learning platforms, library technologies, and security-approved research tools — and identify the gaps that keep academic materials out of reach. On the pedagogical side, I co-design surveys, focus groups, and infrastructural assessments with students and faculty to understand how digital literacy, research strategy, and teaching practice work inside a college-in-prison context, and I build a programmatic knowledge base of approved technologies and offline research resources that people can genuinely use. I partner with the NYU Division of Libraries, the New York State Department of Corrections and Community Supervision (DOCCS), and peer college-in-prison providers on strategic planning and shared resource development, and I develop resources for both currently incarcerated students and formerly incarcerated students continuing their education after release.

Before this, I completed my MSc in Electrical Sciences & Computer Engineering at Brown University, where I was supervised first by Professor Chris Rose and then by Professor Thomas Serre. Earlier, I completed my BSc in Physics at the University of Massachusetts Amherst.

Before starting graduate school at Brown, I worked on developing data engineering best practices and computer vision models for agricultural research at Terramera Inc in Vancouver, BC.
